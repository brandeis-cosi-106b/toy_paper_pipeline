\section{Introduction}

Response time is a critical metric in cognitive psychology and human-computer interaction research \cite{donders1969speed}. Understanding factors that influence decision-making speed has implications for interface design, training protocols, and human performance optimization \cite{wickens2013engineering}.

In this study, we examine whether a brief training intervention can reduce response times in a simple decision task without sacrificing accuracy. We hypothesize that participants receiving task-specific training will demonstrate faster responses compared to a baseline control condition.

\subsection{Methodology}

We recruited 15 participants and randomly assigned them to either a baseline condition (no training) or a treatment condition (5-minute training session). All participants completed the same response-time task, and we measured both accuracy and response latency.
